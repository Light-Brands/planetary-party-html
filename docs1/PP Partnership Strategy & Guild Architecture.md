# PP Partnership Strategy

# **🌿 Planetary Party Partnership Strategy**

*A Strategic Framework for Ecosystem Alignment & Regenerative Growth*  
 (Living Document – Version 0.1)

## **1\. Purpose of This Document**

The Planetary Party is a living coordination ecosystem designed to support bioregional communities through coherence, capital flow, governance innovation, and cultural activation.

As participation expands, we recognize the need to clarify:

* What types of partners we are seeking  
* What strategic priorities guide partnership decisions  
* How partners are evaluated for alignment and ecosystem fit  
* How expansion happens without fragmentation

This document provides a lightweight strategic architecture to guide partnership development in alignment with the Planetary Party’s Theory of Change and current phase of growth.

## **2\. Partnership Philosophy**

The Planetary Party is not building a centralized organization.  
 We are cultivating a trusted coordination ecosystem.

Partnerships are:

* Relational, not extractive  
* Sovereign, not hierarchical  
* Strategic, not opportunistic  
* Phased, not indiscriminate

Trust and vouching remain foundational (as outlined in the Network Governance Proposal), but partnership activation also aligns with ecosystem priorities and capacity.

## **3\. Alignment With Our Theory of Change**

Our Theory of Change identifies the polycrisis as a coordination failure.  
 We support bioregional communities to move through the cycle:

**Sense → See → Flow → Celebrate → Regenerate**

Partnerships are evaluated based on how they strengthen one or more phases of this cycle.

Questions we ask:

* Does this partner strengthen coordination infrastructure?  
* Does this partner unlock or steward capital flow?  
* Does this partner deepen governance coherence?  
* Does this partner amplify regenerative solutions?  
* Does this partner strengthen relational trust?

If not clearly connected to the Theory of Change, the partnership may be premature.

## **4\. Strategic Partner Categories**

Partners land within strategic categories.  
 This helps clarify role, expectations, and integration pathways.

### **A. Bioregional Anchors**

Place-based partners rooted in specific bioregions.

Examples:

* Indigenous councils  
* Land-based regenerative projects  
* Local governance leaders  
* Deployment pod leaders

Role:  
 Activate the protocol locally and steward bioregional sovereignty.

Priority Level:  
 High – Foundational to the ecosystem.

### **B. Infrastructure Partners**

Provide tools, frameworks, or systems the ecosystem leverages.

Examples:

* Technology platforms  
* Governance systems  
* Capital vehicles  
* Mapping tools  
* Media infrastructure

Role:  
 Strengthen coordination capacity without centralizing control.

Priority Level:  
 Phase-dependent (based on infrastructure readiness).

### **C. Capital & Flow Partners**

Enable financial, philanthropic, or regenerative revenue pathways.

Examples:

* Flow funds  
* Fiscal sponsors  
* Impact investors  
* Family offices  
* Regenerative enterprise builders

Role:  
 Unlock capital aligned with bioregional sovereignty and regenerative principles.

Priority Level:  
 Strategically curated – requires strong coherence alignment.

### **D. Cultural Amplifiers**

Shape narrative and visibility.

Examples:

* Storytelling collectives  
* Media platforms  
* Documentary teams  
* Festival collaborators

Role:  
 Strengthen coherence through narrative, celebration, and visibility.

Priority Level:  
 High leverage when aligned with activation arcs.

### **E. Strategic Systems Architects**

Support evolution of governance, coordination, and ecosystem design.

Examples:

* Regenerative governance designers  
* Theory of change architects  
* Ecosystem strategists

Role:  
 Strengthen structural coherence and long-term resilience.

Priority Level:  
 Time-sensitive during structural refinement phases.

### **F. Emergent / Experimental Collaborators**

Innovators exploring potential integration.

Role:  
 Pilot new models and experiments without premature structural embedding.

Priority Level:  
 Limited intake – aligned with capacity.

## **5\. Partnership Evaluation Framework**

Relational vouching remains core.  
 In addition, we evaluate:

### **1\. Strategic Fit**

* Does this partner serve a current priority category?  
* Does this strengthen a weak point in the ecosystem?

### **2\. Timing**

* Is the ecosystem ready for this integration?  
* Do we have stewardship capacity?

### **3\. Sovereignty Respect**

* Does this partner honor bioregional autonomy?  
* Do they attempt to prescribe solutions?

### **4\. Reciprocity & Integrity**

* Are IP, capital, and contributions handled transparently?  
* Does this partner operate from regenerative principles?

### **5\. Brand & Field Integrity**

* Is this partner aligned with PPP’s values?  
* Would public association strengthen or dilute coherence?

## **6\. Phased Partner Priorities (90-Day Cycles)**

At any given time, the Core / Strategy & Coherence Working Group may define:

* Priority partner categories for the next 90 days  
* Categories on hold  
* Capacity thresholds

Example:

Current Phase Focus:

* Bioregional Anchors (Colombia, Jamaica, Northeast)  
* Capital & Flow alignment refinement  
* Governance / Theory of Change coherence

Paused:

* Additional tech infrastructure pilots  
* New festival expansion beyond existing arcs

This ensures expansion aligns with strategic rhythm.

## **7\. Integration & Landing Architecture**

Each partner category has:

* A designated Steward or Working Group  
* A clear onboarding pathway (as defined in onboarding docs)  
* An MOU structure  
* Communication channel alignment  
* IP & reciprocity clarity

No partner enters without a defined landing zone.

## **8\. Levels of Public Association**

To protect field coherence, public association may vary:

* Explorer (learning, no public use of name)  
* Contributor (limited association)  
* Recognized Partner (public alignment)  
* Steward / Orchestrator (represents PPP in ecosystem contexts)

Advancement is based on contribution, trust, and strategic alignment.

## **9\. Growth Guardrails**

To avoid fragmentation:

* 3 vouching team members; transparent vouching lineage  
* Rotating stewardship roles  
* Clear partner categories  
* Strategic priority cycles  
* Brand coherence standards  
* Conflict resolution pathways

Expansion without architecture leads to dilution.  
 Architecture without openness leads to stagnation.  
 We steward the balance.

## **10\. Living Evolution**

This is a living document, we have a living partner strategy.

It evolves through:

* Experimentation  
* Bioregional learning  
* Steward dialogue  
* Ecosystem feedback

Partnership strategy is adaptive — rooted in coherence.

# PP Guild Architecture Framework

# **🌍 Planetary Party Guild Architecture Framework**

*A Living Structure for Planetary Convergence & Operational Design*

## **1\. Why This Framework Exists**

As the Planetary Party ecosystem grows, our Guilds are becoming both:

* Convergence spaces for aligned planetary actors  
* Operational bodies designing and delivering tools, systems, and activations

This document clarifies what a Guild is and what a Guild is not; The two operational layers within each Guild and the types of participation possible, as well as how Guilds relate to strategic partners, the core fractal, and bioregions.

This framework supports coherence without centralization.

## **2\. What Is a Planetary Party Guild?**

A Guild is a **planetary convergence field organized around a core functional domain** necessary for bioregional regeneration.

Guilds exist to:

* Converge aligned actors across organizations and geographies  
* Coordinate intelligence and development of social functions and tools  
* Prototype and refine systems  
* Support bioregional implementation  
* Strengthen planetary coherence in that field

Guilds are living coordination fields

## **3\. The Two Layers Within Each Guild**

Each Guild operates on two interconnected planes:

## **Layer 1: Strategic Planetary Convergence Field**

This layer holds the long-horizon intelligence and alignment of the domain.

It includes:

* Strategic partners  
* Advisory voices  
* Framework stewards  
* Ecosystem architects  
* Organizations aligned at a planetary level

This layer:

* Shapes direction  
* Shares learning across movements  
* Aligns standards and principles  
* Prevents duplication across networks  
* Strengthens coherence of the broader field

Participants in this layer are not required to attend operational meetings.

They may:

* Offer guidance  
* Share frameworks  
* Co-develop standards  
* Align strategy  
* Participate in periodic convergence dialogues

Example:  
 A regenerative finance organization may sit in the Strategic Convergence layer of the Capital Flows Guild without participating in day-to-day participatory budgeting design.

## **Layer 2: Operational Design & Delivery Layer**

This layer builds, tests, maintains, and delivers.

It includes:

* Accountable Stewards  
* Co-Stewards  
* Designers  
* Facilitators  
* Technical implementers  
* Contributors actively executing within the domain

This layer:

* Designs toolkits  
* Builds playbooks  
* Runs pilots  
* Maintains systems  
* Delivers trainings  
* Supports bioregional deployments

Operational participation requires commitment, contribution, and accountability.

## **4\.  Planetary Domains (Guild Convergence Fields)**

The Planetary Party ecosystem is organized around key functional domains necessary for bioregional regeneration.

Each domain is stewarded through a Guild with both a Strategic Convergence Layer and an Operational Design & Delivery Layer.

**4.1 Current Guild Domains**

### **Capacity Building Guild.** Develops training tools, facilitation practices, and relational infrastructure that enable communities to increase cohesion, coherence, and leadership capacity.

### **Events & Activation Guild.** Designs and produces Planetary Party gatherings and activation arcs.  Maintains event playbooks for safety, accessibility, consent culture, and cultural coherence.

### **Governance Frameworks Guild.** Designs and prototypes participatory governance models, civic assembly frameworks, consent-based processes, and decentralized decision-making tools.

### **Storytelling Guild.** Amplifies narratives, manages content flows, and supports communities in sharing their stories across media platforms.

### **Tech Guild.** Designs interoperable tools, dashboards, and digital coordination infrastructure that support transparency, alignment, and planetary-scale collaboration.

### **Living Systems Guild.** Advances ecological regeneration practices across food, soil, water, biodiversity, and waste systems.

### **Capital Flows & Regenerative Finance Guild.** Designs participatory and regenerative capital pathways that channel resources toward bioregional leaders and viable initiatives.

(This includes applied funding mechanisms such as Flow Funding, participatory budgeting, and capital tracking systems. Sovereign trust structures or planetary-scale capital stewards may interface at the Strategic Convergence layer but remain distinct.)

### **Bioregional Intelligence & Dashboard Guild.** Maps ecosystems, acupuncture points, stakeholders, and resource flows across bioregions.  Maintains dashboards that inform decision-making and capital alignment.

### **Community Weaving Guild.** Builds and maintains the relational nervous system of the ecosystem — onboarding, cross-guild coherence, communication channels, and integration rhythms.

# **5\. The Role of the Core Fractal**

The Core Fractal is a coordination body composed of representatives and stewards from across domains.

Its purpose is to:

* Maintain cross-guild coherence  
* Support strategic alignment  
* Resolve cross-domain tensions  
* Steward shared resources  
* Hold the overall integrity of the ecosystem

Guilds retain autonomy within their domain mandate. The Core Fractal supports coherence across domains, not control within them.

## **6\. Participation Types Within Guilds**

Guild participation is not binary.

Participants may engage at different levels:

### **6.1. Strategic Guild Member**

Participates in the Strategic Convergence layer.

* Contributes frameworks, perspective, or institutional alignment  
* Not required to attend operational calls  
* May represent an organization  
* May participate in periodic convergence dialogues

### **6.2. Operational Guild Steward**

Participates in the Design & Delivery layer.

* Holds responsibility for projects  
* Participates consistently  
* May carry decision-making authority within defined scope  
* Commits to defined deliverables

### **6.3. Active Contributor**

* Participates in projects or pilots  
* Offers skills or tools  
* May be time-bound or episodic

### **6.4. Affiliate / Listener**

* Tracks learning  
* Participates occasionally  
* May transition into more active roles over time

# **7\. Earning Decision-Making Power & Voting Rights**

**Purpose:**  
 Ensure shared governance is rooted in contribution, trust, and demonstrated stewardship — not status or self-appointment.

Decision-making authority within the Planetary Party ecosystem is earned through visible participation and delivery.

## **7.1 How Contributors Earn Governance Rights**

Governance participation evolves through:

**1\. Active Participation**  
 Consistent involvement in at least one Guild (Operational layer) over a defined period (e.g., 3+ months).

**2\. Deliverables Completed**  
 Successful creation, facilitation, or implementation of agreed-upon projects or outcomes.

**3\. Values Alignment**  
 Demonstrated collaboration, relational integrity, and commitment to regenerative principles.

**4\. Reciprocal Contribution**  
 Financial, creative, technical, relational, or strategic contributions to the ecosystem.

# **7.2. Decision-Making Tiers**

### **Tier 1: Guild-Level Governance**

Active Operational Contributors may vote on decisions within their Guild’s defined mandate.

Scope:

* Tool development  
* Project prioritization  
* Internal workflow  
* Guild resource allocation within mandate

### **Tier 2: Cross-Guild Representation**

Guild Representatives (selected by their Guild through consent or agreed process) co-decide on:

* Cross-domain matters  
* Shared infrastructure  
* Participatory capital allocations  
* Ecosystem-level initiatives

This layer ensures Guild autonomy while preserving coherence.

### **Tier 3: Ecosystem-Wide Participation**

Contributors may participate in ecosystem-wide decisions after:

* Completing onboarding  
* Demonstrating alignment and delivery  
* Moving through a defined “Bloom Period”

Ecosystem-wide decisions may include:

* Adoption of new protocols  
* Allocation of participatory budgets  
* Evolution of structural governance  
* Strategic shifts affecting all domains

## **8\. Guild Governance**

Each Guild includes:

* An Accountable Steward (holds decision authority within mandate)  
* A Co-Steward (supports coherence and reflection)  
* Clear domain mandate  
* Defined scope of authority  
* Defined relationship to the Strategy & Coherence Working Group

Guild autonomy is respected within domain scope.

Escalation occurs only when:

* Cross-domain impacts arise  
* Governance coherence is affected  
* Conflict cannot be resolved internally

## **9\. Relationship Between Guilds & Strategic Partners**

Not all strategic partners are operational guild members.

An organization may:

* Sit in the Strategic Convergence layer of a Guild  
* Partner at a planetary level without operational involvement  
* Collaborate in specific bioregional deployments  
* Offer tools discoverable by operational guild members

This prevents forced operational commitments while preserving coherence.

## **10\. Relationship to Bioregions**

Guilds serve bioregions. They do not control them.

Guilds:

* Offer tools  
* Provide frameworks  
* Support coordination  
* Share learning across regions

Bioregions retain sovereignty over adoption and adaptation.

## **11\. Relationship to Capital & Sovereign Trust Structures**

Operational Guilds may design applied mechanisms.

Planetary-level trust structures (e.g., sovereign trusts, large-scale capital stewards) may interface at the Strategic Convergence layer.

Applied financial tools ≠ sovereign trust stewardship.

These layers remain distinct but aligned.

## **12\. Living Evolution**

Guild architecture evolves.

As participation grows:

* Roles may refine  
* Governance patterns may mature  
* Participation types may expand

All evolution remains grounded in:

* Bioregional sovereignty  
* Regenerative principles  
* Relational trust  
* Strategic coherence
